Stem Removal by Latent Subtraction

Audio examples for “What Makes Audio Latents Mixing-Equivariant? A Controlled Study of Explicit Supervision” (ICASSP 2027 submission) · code & paper. Each example removes one stem from a MUSDB18 test mixture entirely in latent space: g(f(mix) − f(stem)). No separation model is involved — only the autoencoder’s latent arithmetic.

Protocol.
  • Example selection is deterministic and independent of every model’s output: windows are chosen on a fixed grid by the energy of the stem being removed. No cherry-picking on results.
  • Clips are consecutive 1-second chunks processed independently (the paper’s protocol), joined with a -sample linear crossfade. Mono, 44.1 kHz.
  • All files within an example share one gain, so relative levels between mixture, stem, target, and model outputs are truthful.
  • “Ceiling” is the model’s plain encode–decode of the true residual — the best that model could sound with no latent arithmetic at all. SI-SDR is computed against the true residual over the whole clip.
