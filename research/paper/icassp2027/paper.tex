\title{Latent Arithmetic Measures the Origin:\\Recentering Audio Autoencoders Without Retraining}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Nurdaulet Akhanov}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence\\
Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates\\
nurdaulet.akhanov@mbzuai.ac.ae}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Audio latents are increasingly judged by arithmetic: interpolate two codes and
decode a mix, subtract a stem's code and decode the residual. We show that the
second probe is not a property of the autoencoder alone. Shifting the latent
origin by the encoding of silence, $f(0)$, leaves reconstruction and every
unit-sum latent combination exactly unchanged for arbitrary nonlinear encoders
and decoders, yet moves stem-subtraction scores by decibels. On five fixed
checkpoints and a pre-registered held-out corpus of 240 recordings, the shift
removes $87$--$95$\% of the apparent subtraction advantage of mixing
supervision. Applied to autoencoders we did not train, including DAC, EnCodec
and Music2Latent, the same shift improves stem subtraction by TBD--TBD\,dB
with no retraining. What mixing supervision genuinely buys is convex mixing,
which the shift provably cannot touch: $+1.8$ and $+1.4$\,dB at two
compression rates, replicated across training seeds. Latent arithmetic should
be reported against a declared origin.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio autoencoders, latent arithmetic, representation learning,
neural audio codecs, evaluation methodology
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Mixing is linear on waveforms,
$\mathrm{mix}(x_1,x_2,\alpha)=\alpha x_1+(1{-}\alpha)x_2$, which makes latent
arithmetic an attractive interface to a learned audio representation. For an
encoder $f$ and decoder $g$, the property usually asked for is
\begin{equation}
  g\!\left(\alpha f(x_1)+(1{-}\alpha)f(x_2)\right)\approx \alpha x_1+(1{-}\alpha)x_2 ,
  \label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}
and a common companion probe is stem removal by subtraction,
$g(f(x_\mathrm{mix}){-}f(x_\mathrm{stem}))\approx x_\mathrm{res}$. Where these
hold, editing happens in the latent domain without a separation model, and
independently generated latents can be composed by addition, one route open to
latent-diffusion systems \cite{liu2023audioldm,evans2024stable}.
Reconstruction objectives do not enforce Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv}, and Torres et
al.~\cite{torres2026linearity} induce it in a consistency autoencoder by
decoding averaged latents against the true mix.

This paper asks a prior question: \emph{what do these arithmetic scores
measure?} The two probes differ in one respect that is easy to overlook. The
coefficients in Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv} sum to one; those of subtraction sum to
zero. A latent space is defined only up to a choice of origin, and a shift of
that origin cancels in the first case but not in the second.
\secref{sec:origin} makes this exact for arbitrary nonlinear $f$ and $g$:
recentering the latent space at $f(0)$ leaves the autoencoding function and
every unit-sum combination pointwise identical, while mapping subtraction to
$g(f(x_\mathrm{mix}){-}f(x_\mathrm{stem}){+}f(0))$. Any change a subtraction
score shows under this map is therefore a property of the coordinate system,
not of the model as a function. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A reparameterization argument (\secref{sec:origin}) showing that
  subtraction-style latent arithmetic is coordinate-dependent while
  reconstruction and convex mixing are not, and identifying the model's own
  reconstruction of the target as the coordinate-invariant reference.
  \item The intervention on five fixed checkpoints of a waveform GAN
  autoencoder (\secref{sec:subtraction}), on the test set and on a
  pre-registered held-out corpus: recentering removes $87$--$95$\% of the
  advantage that mixing supervision appears to have on stem subtraction and
  lands every checkpoint within half a decibel of its own reconstruction.
  \item The same shift applied to autoencoders we did not train
  (\secref{sec:codecs}): DAC \cite{kumar2024dac}, EnCodec
  \cite{defossez2023encodec}, Music2Latent \cite{pasini2024music2latent} and
  the public baselines of \cite{torres2026linearity}. Recentering improves
  stem subtraction on every one of them without retraining, including the
  linearity-trained model, whose offset is ten times smaller than its
  baseline's but not zero.
  \item What supervision genuinely buys (\secref{sec:mixing}): convex mixing,
  which the intervention cannot affect, improves by $+1.8$ and $+1.4$\,dB at
  two compression rates, replicates across training seeds, and reduces level
  error; the widely used decode-versus-decode score is inflated by an
  adversarial term and by decoder noise (\secref{sec:protocol}).
\end{enumerate}
The practical reading is a two-line recipe: recentre at $f(0)$ before
subtracting, whatever the model; add decode-mixing supervision if convex
mixing is the goal.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Neural audio codecs.} DAC \cite{kumar2024dac}, EnCodec
\cite{defossez2023encodec} and SoundStream \cite{zeghidour2022soundstream}
pair convolutional encoders with HiFi-GAN-style decoders \cite{kong2020hifigan}
and multi-scale discriminators, optimizing reconstruction without latent
structure; their residual vector quantization is a further obstacle to
interpolation, so we operate on their continuous pre-quantization latents.
RAVE \cite{caillon2021rave} learns a variational latent space but does not
target mixing equivariance, and disentanglement regularizers
\cite{higgins2017betavae} encourage generic smoothness rather than a specific
identity. Mixing-equivariance follows the symmetry-based view of
representation learning \cite{higgins2018towards}, though convex mixing is an
affine combination, not a group action.
\textbf{Linear audio representations.} Music2Latent (M2L)
\cite{pasini2024music2latent} is a consistency autoencoder with partial
linearity as a byproduct. Torres et al.~\cite{torres2026linearity} make it
approximately linear (Lin-CAE) by decoding the average of separately encoded
latents against the true mix and by a gain augmentation that enforces
homogeneity, a principle related to mixup \cite{zhang2018mixup}. Their
oracle-separation scores subtract raw latents and compare two decodes, a
protocol \secref{sec:codecs} and \secref{sec:protocol} examine. No prior work
reports latent arithmetic against a declared origin.

\section{The Latent Origin Is a Free Coordinate}\label{sec:origin}
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.} Let $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}$ and
$g:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{X}$ be arbitrary maps and $c=f(0)$ the encoding of
silence. Define the recentered pair
\begin{equation}
  f_c(x)=f(x)-c, \qquad g_c(z)=g(z+c).
  \label{eq:recenter}
\end{equation}
Then (i) $g_c(f_c(x))=g(f(x))$ for every $x$; (ii) for any $x_1,\dots,x_n$
and coefficients with $\sum_i a_i=1$,
$g_c(\sum_i a_i f_c(x_i))=g(\sum_i a_i f(x_i))$; and (iii) for the
coefficients $(1,-1)$,
\begin{equation}
  g_c\!\left(f_c(x)-f_c(y)\right)=g\!\left(f(x)-f(y)+f(0)\right).
  \label{eq:sub}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} $f_c(x)+c=f(x)$ gives (i). For (ii),
$\sum_i a_i f_c(x_i)=\sum_i a_i f(x_i)-c\sum_i a_i=\sum_i a_i f(x_i)-c$, and
$g_c$ adds $c$ back. For (iii) the coefficients sum to zero, so $c$ cancels
in the latent difference and $g_c$ adds it inside $g$. $\square$

Three consequences shape the paper. First, $(f_c,g_c)$ is \emph{the same
autoencoder} as $(f,g)$ as far as reconstruction and Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv} are
concerned: by (i) and (ii) those quantities are pointwise identical, for
arbitrary nonlinear networks, with no retraining and no approximation.
Second, by (iii) subtraction is not: recentering shifts it by one encode of
silence, and \secref{sec:subtraction} measures a several-decibel shift. A
model ranking obtained from raw subtraction can therefore be altered by a
change of coordinates that provably leaves reconstruction and mixing
untouched, so such a ranking is not by itself a statement about the
representation. Third, when $f$ is affine, $f(x)=Ax+b$, we have
$f(x){-}f(y){+}f(0)=f(x{-}y)$ and Eq.~\ref{eq:sub} decodes exactly
$g(f(x_\mathrm{res}))$, the model's own reconstruction of the residual,
whatever the decoder does. That encode--decode reconstruction is the
reference we report against; the shortfall from it is the \emph{linearity
tax}. Real encoders depart from affine, so for them any improvement from
recentering is an empirical finding; homogeneity $f(ax)=af(x)$, if trained
for, drives $f(0)$ toward zero but need not remove the effect.
The proposition concerns re-expressing a \emph{frozen} model: it does not
make training origin-invariant, and a latent $\ell_2$ penalty in particular
can move $f(0)$.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Models}\label{sec:models}
\textbf{Our checkpoints.} The primary model is a waveform autoencoder
(DAC-style 1-D convolutional encoder, HiFi-GAN decoder \cite{kong2020hifigan},
multi-scale-STFT and multi-period discriminators) at 44.1\,kHz on 1-second
mono chunks, trained on a ${\sim}$950-hour union of FMA-large, MAESTRO and
MUSDB18-HQ \cite{rafii2017musdb18}.\footnote{Code, configurations, weights,
every evaluation record and audio examples:
\url{https://github.com/nurdauletakhanov/musicgen}} Two compression rates are
used: $7.66\times$ ($d{=}128$) and $15.3\times$ ($d{=}64$). Mixing
supervision is the explicit-loss form of the additivity principle of
\cite{torres2026linearity}: pairs $(x_i,x_j)$ from a fixed-point-free
permutation, $\alpha\sim U[0,1]$,
$\bar{x}=\alpha x_i{+}(1{-}\alpha)x_j$, $\bar{z}=\alpha f(x_i){+}(1{-}\alpha)f(x_j)$,
and
\begin{equation}
  \Ldec=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}\!\left(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x}\right)
        +\lVert g(\bar{z})-\bar{x}\rVert_1 ,
  \label{eq:ldec}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}$ is the multi-resolution STFT and mel loss
also used for reconstruction; the mixed path carries the extra unweighted
waveform $\ell_1$ term, the reconstruction path does not. Gradients flow
through $g$ and into $f$. The total objective is
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}+\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{GAN}+\lambda\Ldec
+\lambda_{\ell_2}\lVert z\rVert^2$ with $\lambda{=}0.5$ and
$\lambda_{\ell_2}{=}10^{-3}$; a variant also applies the discriminator to the
mixed path (``$+$disc''). At $7.66\times$ the mixing arms are 25k-step
fine-tunes of a converged reconstruction-only checkpoint against a
continued-training control; at $15.3\times$ the combined $\Ldec{+}$disc
recipe and its control are trained from scratch for 250k steps under matched
configurations. The five resulting checkpoints, plus the reconstruction-only
ancestor, are frozen for everything below.

\textbf{Third-party autoencoders.} We apply the intervention, unchanged, to
public models we had no hand in: DAC at 44.1\,kHz \cite{kumar2024dac} and
EnCodec at 24\,kHz \cite{defossez2023encodec}, using their continuous encoder
outputs with the quantizer bypassed on every path; the published Music2Latent
checkpoint \cite{pasini2024music2latent}; and the three public models of
\cite{torres2026linearity}: their M2L retrained on their corpus, Lin-CAE,
and Lin-CAE-2. None of these received any change of weights. EnCodec is
resampled to and from 24\,kHz, which band-limits every route equally.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
SI-SDR \cite{leroux2019sdr} is the waveform score throughout. Stem removal is
\emph{oracle}: the removed stem is available and encoded, so the probe tests
the representation's arithmetic, not blind separation. Raw subtraction
decodes $f(x_\mathrm{mix}){-}f(x_\mathrm{stem})$; the recentered form adds
$f(0)$ as in Eq.~\ref{eq:sub}. Both are scored against the true residual,
and against each model's own reconstruction of it (the reference of
\secref{sec:origin}); the difference is the linearity tax. For consistency
decoders, which draw fresh noise per call and never align in phase with a raw
waveform, we report the decode-versus-decode form with shared noise, as
\cite{torres2026linearity} do against autoencoded ground truth. For convex
mixing at $\alpha{=}0.5$ we report
SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}=\mathrm{SISDR}(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x})$
(against the true mix), SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}=\mathrm{SISDR}(g(\bar{z}),g(f(\bar{x})))$
(decode versus decode), reconstruction SI-SDR$_\mathrm{rec}$, and MixRate, the
ratio of the mixed-path loss of Eq.~\ref{eq:ldec} for $g(\bar{z})$ to that
for $g(f(\bar{x}))$ ($<1$ beats the encode-then-decode path). FAD uses
music-finetuned LAION-CLAP embeddings of 10\,s windows at 48\,kHz and is not
comparable with VGGish FAD.

\subsection{Protocol and what the intervals cover}\label{sec:protocol-setup}
Subtraction uses 49 of the 50 MUSDB18 test tracks (one is excluded because
its stems sum to the mixture at only $18.5$\,dB), 30 windows per track, four
stems: 5{,}880 (track, stem, chunk) units. Window selection is a pure
function of the seed and the sorted track list, so every model, ours or not,
sees identical units and every contrast is paired. Held-out evaluation uses
MoisesDB \cite{pereira2023moisesdb}, 240 recordings absent from the training
data and from checkpoint selection of every model evaluated on it, under a
protocol registered before the audio was downloaded, with frozen weights and
verified hashes; a unit is scored where the removed stem is active
($24{,}648$ of $28{,}800$). MUSDB18 is training data for DAC and MoisesDB is
training data for the Lin-CAE family, so each third-party model is reported
on the corpus it has not seen. Confidence intervals come from a paired
cluster bootstrap over recordings ($10^4$ resamples); mixing pairs join two
chunks, so those intervals weight a pair by the multiplicity of each member.
Intervals are a conditional diagnostic: they cover evaluation variance for
these checkpoints and cover neither training-seed variance nor checkpoint
selection. That selection needs disclosure: our released weights are the
lowest-validation-loss checkpoint and the validation loader read the test
split. For every $7.66\times$ run this coincides with the final step; both
$15.3\times$ runs were selected at step 240k of 250k. The origin intervention
compares a checkpoint with itself, so it is unaffected; between-model
contrasts are why the held-out corpus exists.

\textbf{In raw coordinates the supervision looks decisive.} It raises
subtraction from $+3.4$ to $+5.8$\,dB at $7.66\times$ and from $+1.2$ to
$+5.2$\,dB at $15.3\times$ (\tabref{tab:subtraction}).

\textbf{Recentering removes most of it.} Adding $f(0)$ gains $+2.0$ to
$+6.0$\,dB for every configuration and leaves each within $0.4$--$0.5$\,dB of
its own reconstruction reference; the models that gain most looked worst. The
between-model advantage falls to $-0.03$, $-0.07$ and $+0.01$\,dB, all three
intervals containing zero (\tabref{tab:origin}), and the reduction holds on
$49$ of $49$ tracks in every pair. The no-mixing models
carry about three times the offset ($\lVert f(0)\rVert$ $18.1$ versus $6.6$
at $7.66\times$, $14.8$ versus $5.4$ at $15.3\times$): much of what mixing
supervision did to raw subtraction was to shrink an offset that a change of
coordinates removes for free.

\textbf{Held-out replication, and a residue.} On MoisesDB, raw subtraction
again favours the mixing models by $+2.3$ to $+4.5$\,dB and recentering
removes $87$--$95$\% of that, shrinking the gap on $229$, $231$ and $236$ of
the 240 recordings (\tabref{tab:origin}); on the rest it overshoots and
reverses the sign. The residue is resolvable here, $+0.29$, $+0.12$ and
$+0.42$\,dB with intervals excluding zero, where the 49-track set could not.
Recentering lifts each held-out score to within $0.5$--$1.0$\,dB of its
reference.


\textbf{Baselines without latent arithmetic.} The unchanged mixture scores
$+17.1$\,dB against the residual, above every model's reconstruction
reference, because the removed stem is a small share of the energy: the
metric certifies arithmetic relative to that reference, not removal.
Recentered latent subtraction beats decode-then-subtract,
$g(f(x_\mathrm{mix})){-}g(f(x_\mathrm{stem}))$, by $+0.7$ to $+0.9$\,dB; raw
subtraction loses to it everywhere.

The proposition is model-agnostic, so we apply the shift to autoencoders with

subtraction quality that the coordinate change does not, and the coordinate
change buys quality that the training does not: both models improve, and the
gap between them does not close.

\subsection{Convex mixing tells a different story}\label{sec:mixing}
By part (ii) of the proposition, recentering cannot change any quantity in
Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv}. The mixing probe therefore reports on the autoencoder
itself, and there the supervision does something. On the held-out corpus
$\Ldec$ improves ground-truth mixing SI-SDR by $+1.77$\,dB $[+1.73,+1.81]$ at
$7.66\times$ and the combined recipe by $+1.39$\,dB $[+1.33,+1.45]$ at
$15.3\times$, at reconstruction changes of $+0.04$ and $-0.07$\,dB. On the
test set $\Ldec$ raises SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ from $8.0$ to
$10.2$\,dB and is the only configuration with MixRate below one ($0.97$
against $1.20$), with reconstruction SI-SDR spanning $11.2$--$11.4$\,dB and
FAD $0.044$--$0.046$ across all fine-tunes. An independent re-run of the
control and of $\Ldec$ from the same ancestor gives $8.1$ and $10.3$\,dB, so
the $2.2$\,dB test-set gain replicates across training seeds. From scratch at
$15.3\times$ the combined recipe improves $6.8$ to $8.6$\,dB and MixRate
$1.19$ to $1.05$ at equal reconstruction, while FAD rises from $0.052$ to
$0.061$. Across a five-point $\alpha$ sweep the gain holds at every
coefficient and the SI-SDR range shrinks from $3.2$ to $1.9$\,dB.
\textbf{Level, not only shape:} the gain SI-SDR discards shows the decoded
mix $1.6$\,dB too quiet without the loss and $0.9$\,dB with it. Decoder-only
adaptation with a frozen encoder retains $9.5$ of the $10.2$\,dB, and
encoder-side supervision $\Lenc=\lVert f(\bar{x})-\bar{z}\rVert^2$ lowers the
latent error but reaches only $8.4$--$8.7$\,dB: the property lives in the
decoder's treatment of interpolated latents.
\textbf{The mixed-path discriminator buys the metric, not the property.} At
$7.66\times$ it raises decode-versus-decode SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$ from
$12.1$ to $16.0$\,dB while ground-truth mixing falls from $10.2$ to $9.9$\,dB
and MixRate rises to $1.05$; held out, agreement rises by $+3.98$\,dB while
fidelity falls by $0.49$\,dB $[-0.51,-0.48]$. The two measurements rank these
checkpoints differently and are not interchangeable evidence.

\subsection{Decode-versus-decode scores are protocol-sensitive}\label{sec:protocol}
For stochastic decoders the decode-versus-decode metric is confounded a
second way. A consistency-model decoder \cite{pasini2024music2latent} draws
fresh noise per call: decoding the \emph{same} latent twice is deterministic
under shared noise but scores $-1.8$\,dB under independent noise, with no
latent arithmetic at all. On identical pairs the same two checkpoints score
$-9.1$ and $-7.3$\,dB with independent noise and $+3.6$ and $+5.9$\,dB when
they share it: a $13$\,dB swing driven by the protocol rather than the model.
Which protocol prior work used is not stated in enough detail for us to
determine. We share decode noise everywhere and, for deterministic decoders,
score against the true waveform.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Latent stem subtraction is measured in coordinates that the model never
fixes. Recentering at the encoded silence leaves the autoencoding function
and every unit-sum combination pointwise unchanged, for arbitrary nonlinear
encoders and decoders, yet it moves subtraction scores by several decibels:
it removes $87$--$95$\% of the advantage mixing supervision appeared to have
on a pre-registered 240-track corpus, and it improves stem subtraction on
DAC, EnCodec, Music2Latent and our own reconstruction-only model with no
retraining. What the supervision buys is convex equivariance, which the
intervention cannot affect and which improves at both rates and across
seeds. Two practices follow: report latent arithmetic against a declared
origin and against the model's own reconstruction of the target, and recentre
before subtracting, whatever the model.
\textbf{Limitations.} Recovery is exact only for an affine encoder; for the
networks here the gain is empirical and leaves a residue. Comparisons use
single training runs except where stated; intervals cover recording-level
evaluation variance for fixed checkpoints, not variation across training
runs. Selection used the test split; audio is mono, subtraction is oracle,
and no listening test was run.
