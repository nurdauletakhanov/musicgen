\title{A Controlled Study of Mixing Supervision\\and Latent Arithmetic in Audio Autoencoders}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Nurdaulet Akhanov}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence\\
Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates\\
nurdaulet.akhanov@mbzuai.ac.ae}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Explicit mixing supervision aims to improve audio composition through latent
arithmetic, but common evaluations measure different properties of an
autoencoder. We investigate these effects through same-start fine-tuning
comparisons in GAN audio autoencoders. Decode-mixing supervision improves
ground-truth convex mixing with little change in reconstruction SI-SDR. An
independent held-out corpus confirms a $1.77$\,dB mixing improvement at
$7.66\times$ compression and $1.39$\,dB for a combined recipe at
$15.3\times$. Discriminator supervision, however, increases decoder
consistency while reducing ground-truth mixing fidelity. For source
subtraction, correcting the latent origin removes $87$--$95$\% of the raw
between-model advantage on 240 held-out recordings. These results distinguish waveform mixing gains from decoder agreement and
from origin-dependent subtraction effects. Findings are conditional on single
training runs; held-out evaluation uses frozen checkpoints that were selected
on the original evaluation data.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio autoencoders, latent arithmetic, evaluation methodology,
representation learning, controlled ablation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Audio mixing is linear on waveforms,
$\mathrm{mix}(x_1,x_2,\alpha)=\alpha x_1+(1{-}\alpha)x_2$, which makes latent
arithmetic an attractive interface to a learned representation. For an
encoder $f$ and decoder $g$, the property usually asked for is
\begin{equation}
  g\!\left(\alpha f(x_1)+(1{-}\alpha)f(x_2)\right)\approx \alpha x_1+(1{-}\alpha)x_2 ,
  \label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}
and a common companion probe is stem removal by subtraction,
$g(f(x_\mathrm{mix}){-}f(x_\mathrm{stem}))\approx x_\mathrm{res}$. Where these hold, editing happens in the latent domain without a dedicated
separation or synthesis model, and composing independently generated latents
by direct arithmetic, one route open to latent-diffusion systems
\cite{liu2023audioldm,evans2024stable}, requires exactly this property. Standard reconstruction objectives do not enforce Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv}: they
reward fidelity, not algebraic structure, and in
quantized codecs (DAC \cite{kumar2024dac}, EnCodec
\cite{defossez2023encodec}) an interpolated point is not a codebook entry.

\textbf{The supervision studied here is not ours.} Torres et
al.~\cite{torres2026linearity} induce linearity in a consistency autoencoder
by decoding the average of separately encoded latents against the true mix, a
principle related to mixup \cite{zhang2018mixup}. We write that same
supervision as an explicit loss on a waveform GAN autoencoder. The
implementation differs from theirs in loss family (multi-resolution STFT, mel
and a waveform $\ell_1$ term rather than a consistency loss), in mixing
coefficient ($\alpha\sim U[0,1]$ rather than $0.5$), and in decoder class, so
this is neither a new method nor an exact reproduction. What we contribute is
the controlled comparison and the analysis of what the standard measurements
actually report:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Supervision comparison.} Same-start fine-tuning shows how
  decode-mixing supervision, an encoder-side mixing loss and decoder-only
  adaptation affect ground-truth mixing and reconstruction
  (\secref{sec:mixing}, \secref{sec:training}).
  \item \textbf{Evaluation comparison.} Adding discriminator supervision on
  the mixed path improves agreement between the two decoded outputs while
  reducing ground-truth mixing fidelity, so the two measurements favour
  different models and are not interchangeable evidence
  (\secref{sec:consistency}).
  \item \textbf{Origin analysis.} A short argument shows that recentering the
  latent leaves reconstruction and every unit-sum combination pointwise
  unchanged while altering subtraction, and empirically the correction
  substantially reduces between-model subtraction differences, including on
  an independent corpus (\secref{sec:origin}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Neural audio codecs.} DAC \cite{kumar2024dac},
EnCodec \cite{defossez2023encodec} and SoundStream \cite{zeghidour2022soundstream}
pair convolutional encoders with HiFi-GAN decoders \cite{kong2020hifigan} and
multi-scale discriminators, optimizing reconstruction without latent
structure; under their residual vector quantization an interpolated point is
generally not a codebook entry.
RAVE \cite{caillon2021rave} learns a variational latent space but does not
target mixing equivariance, and disentanglement regularizers
\cite{higgins2017betavae} encourage generic smoothness rather than a specific
algebraic identity. Mixing-equivariance follows the symmetry-based view of
representation learning \cite{higgins2018towards}, though convex mixing is an
affine combination, not a group action.
\textbf{Linear audio representations.} Music2Latent
\cite{pasini2024music2latent} is a consistency autoencoder with partial
linearity as a byproduct, and Torres et al.~\cite{torres2026linearity} make it
approximately linear by the supervision described above. Their additivity and
separation scores compare two decodes, a protocol \secref{sec:metrics} shows
to be sensitive to decoder sampling.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Mixing supervision}\label{sec:loss}
Given a batch $\{x_i\}$ we form pairs $(x_i,x_j)$ with a fixed-point-free
permutation and draw $\alpha\sim U[0,1]$. With $\bar{x}=\alpha
x_i+(1{-}\alpha)x_j$ and $\bar{z}=\alpha f(x_i)+(1{-}\alpha)f(x_j)$, the
decode-mixing loss requires the decoded interpolation to match the true mix:
\begin{equation}
  \Ldec=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}\!\left(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x}\right)
        +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{wave}\text{-}\ell_1}\!\left(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x}\right),
  \label{eq:ldec}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}$ is the weighted multi-resolution STFT and
mel loss also used for reconstruction and
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{wave}\text{-}\ell_1}$ is the unweighted mean absolute
error over all samples and batch elements. The reconstruction path carries no
waveform term, so the two paths differ in objective. Gradients flow through
$g$ and into $f$ via $\bar{z}$, so one term constrains both networks. We also
study an encoder-only variant, $\Lenc=\lVert f(\bar{x})-\bar{z}\rVert^2$, and
a variant that additionally applies the discriminator to the mixed path
(``$+$disc''). The total objective is
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}+\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{GAN}+\lambda\Ldec
+\lambda_\mathrm{enc}\Lenc+\lambda_{\ell_2}\lVert z\rVert^2$, and the arms
are exclusive. The baseline sets $\lambda{=}\lambda_\mathrm{enc}{=}0$; the
$\Ldec$ arms set $\lambda{=}0.5$ with $\lambda_\mathrm{enc}{=}0$; the
encoder-only arms set $\lambda{=}0$ and sweep $\lambda_\mathrm{enc}$, written
$\gamma$ in \tabref{tab:ablation}, over $5$, $10$ and $20$; the discriminator
reaches the mixed path only in the $+$disc arms and in the $15.3\times$
recipe; the frozen-encoder arm is $\Ldec$ with $f$ held fixed. Throughout
$\lambda_{\ell_2}{=}10^{-3}$, the STFT and mel weights are equal, the
adversarial weight is $0.5$ and feature matching $2$. Training uses Adam at
$10^{-4}$ ($5{\times}10^{-5}$ for the discriminator), $\beta{=}(0.8,0.99)$,
weight decay $10^{-3}$, gradient clipping $1.0$, batch $32$ with two
accumulation steps and $500$ warmup steps.

\subsection{The latent origin is a free coordinate}\label{sec:prop}
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.} For arbitrary $f,g$ and $c=f(0)$, define
$f_c(x)=f(x)-c$ and $g_c(z)=g(z+c)$. Then $g_c(f_c(x))=g(f(x))$ for every
$x$; for any coefficients with $\sum_i a_i=1$,
$g_c(\sum_i a_i f_c(x_i))=g(\sum_i a_i f(x_i))$; and
\begin{equation}
  g_c\!\left(f_c(x)-f_c(y)\right)=g\!\left(f(x)-f(y)+c\right).
  \label{eq:sub}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} $f_c(x)+c=f(x)$ gives the first. For the second,
$\sum_i a_i f_c(x_i)=\sum_i a_i f(x_i)-c$ because the coefficients sum to
one, and $g_c$ adds $c$ back. For the third the coefficients sum to zero, so
$c$ cancels in the difference and $g_c$ adds it inside $g$. $\square$

Reconstruction and every unit-sum combination are therefore pointwise
unchanged by this relabelling, for arbitrary nonlinear networks, while
subtraction is not. Any difference a subtraction score shows under it is a
property of the coordinate system rather than of the autoencoder as a
function. When $f$ is affine, $f(x){-}f(y){+}c=f(x{-}y)$, so
Eq.~\ref{eq:sub} decodes $g(f(x_\mathrm{res}))$, the model's own
reconstruction of the residual, and we report the shortfall from that
reference as the \emph{linearity tax}. The proposition is general, but it concerns re-expressing a \emph{frozen}
model: it does not make training origin-invariant, and the latent $\ell_2$
penalty in particular depends on the origin, so the objective can itself
move $f(0)$. The magnitude of the effect in \secref{sec:origin} is an
empirical finding about the evaluated checkpoints and corpora, and neither
it nor improved subtraction quality follows from the algebra for arbitrary
models.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
We report reconstruction SI-SDR$_\mathrm{rec}=\mathrm{SISDR}(g(f(x)),x)$ and,
at $\alpha{=}0.5$, two views of Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv}:
SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}=\mathrm{SISDR}(g(\bar{z}),g(f(\bar{x})))$
(decode-vs-decode, relative to the model's own reconstruction of the mix) and
SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}=\mathrm{SISDR}(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x})$ (vs.\
ground truth, comparable across models). We also report MixRate
$=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{mix}(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x})/\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{mix}(g(f(\bar{x})),\bar{x})$
with $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{mix}$
the three-term loss of Eq.~\ref{eq:ldec} in both terms, and the latent error
$r=\bar{z}-f(\bar{x})$. The $\ell_\mathrm{lat}$ column of
\tabref{tab:ablation} is the customary ratio $\lVert r\rVert^2/\lVert
f(\bar{x})\rVert^2$, which \secref{sec:training} shows to be
\emph{translation-sensitive}; every latent quantity is a per-sample mean
over the evaluated units. SI-SDR fits a gain before scoring, so we report that gain
separately, on 3{,}000 samples. FAD uses music-finetuned LAION-CLAP
embeddings of non-overlapping 10\,s windows at 48\,kHz, not VGGish, so values
are not comparable with VGGish FAD.

SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$ shares the decode-vs-decode structure of prior
additivity metrics \cite{torres2026linearity} and is additionally sensitive
to decoder sampling. A consistency-model decoder
\cite{pasini2024music2latent} draws fresh noise per call: decoding the
\emph{same} latent twice is deterministic under shared noise but scores
$-1.8$\,dB under independent noise, with no latent arithmetic performed. On
identical pairs the same checkpoints score $-9.1$ and $-7.3$\,dB when the two
decodes draw independent noise and $+3.6$ and $+5.9$\,dB when they share it,
a $13$\,dB swing driven by the protocol rather than the model; which protocol
prior work used is not stated in enough detail for us to determine. We treat
SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ and MixRate as the metrics of record and
share decode noise everywhere.

\subsection{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}
The primary model is a waveform autoencoder (DAC-style 1-D convolutional
encoder, HiFi-GAN decoder \cite{kong2020hifigan}, combined multi-scale-STFT
and multi-period discriminator) at 44.1\,kHz on 1-second mono chunks, trained
on a ${\sim}$950-hour union of FMA-large, MAESTRO and MUSDB18-HQ
\cite{rafii2017musdb18}.\footnote{Code, training configurations, pretrained
weights, every metric reported here, and audio examples:
\url{https://github.com/nurdauletakhanov/musicgen}} Two compression regimes
are used: $7.66\times$ ($d{=}128$) and $15.3\times$ ($d{=}64$). Mixing losses
are added either as a 25k-step fine-tune from a shared converged baseline or
from scratch over 250k steps, each against a matched control that differs
only in the mixing terms. \textbf{Checkpoint selection.} The released weights
are the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss, and the validation loader
read the test split; for every fine-tune this coincides with the final step,
while both from-scratch runs were selected at step 240k of 250k. This is why
\secref{sec:origin} repeats the key measurement on a corpus that took no part
in selection.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Mixing supervision improves ground-truth mixing}\label{sec:mixing}
\tabref{tab:ablation} compares same-start fine-tunes at $7.66\times$. On the
ground-truth metric, $\Ldec$ improves SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ from
$8.0$ to $10.2$\,dB and is the only configuration with MixRate $<1$ ($0.97$).
Reconstruction is similar to the matched control at this rate,
SDR$_\mathrm{rec}$ spanning $11.2$--$11.4$\,dB and FAD $0.044$--$0.046$;
these are single runs, so an absence this small is not established beyond
seed variance.

The improvement is confirmed on audio that took no part in training or in
checkpoint selection. On 240 held-out MoisesDB recordings \cite{pereira2023moisesdb}, $\Ldec$
gains $+1.77$\,dB $[+1.73,+1.81]$ at $7.66\times$ and the combined recipe
$+1.39$ $[+1.33,+1.45]$ at $15.3\times$. All held-out mixing figures come from one evaluation of $14{,}064$ units,
since pairing depends on the batching.
Intervals resample recordings; mixing pairs join two chunks, about $80\%$ of
them from the same recording. A cross-recording pair enters a resample $m_i
m_j$ times and a within-recording pair $m_i$ times, $m$ being that
recording's multiplicity. Across $\alpha$, swept against its matched control, the swing is $1.9$\,dB
with $\Ldec$ against $3.2$ without, and $\Ldec$ leads at all five
coefficients. \textbf{The gain largely corresponds to a reduced penalty for decoding
interpolated latents.} Scoring both routes against the same target on the same units, the direct
route $g(f(\bar{x}))$ moves by $+0.04$\,dB $[+0.03,+0.05]$ under $\Ldec$
while the penalty for routing through the interpolated latent decreases by
$1.73$ $[1.70,1.77]$, from $1.80$ to $0.07$; the two sum to the $+1.77$ gain
by construction. At $15.3\times$ the direct route changes by $-0.14$
$[-0.17,-0.10]$ and the penalty decreases by $1.52$ $[1.48,1.58]$, so there
the reconstruction change is not negligible.

\subsection{What the training comparisons establish}\label{sec:training}
The encoder-only variant $\Lenc$ drives $\ell_\mathrm{lat}$ lowest
($0.034$--$0.044$) and leaves MixRate high, and it yields smaller
improvements in ground-truth waveform mixing than decode-mixing supervision
($8.4$--$8.7$\,dB against $10.2$, from $8.0$). Training only the decoder under $\Ldec$, with the encoder frozen, retains
most of the gain ($9.5$\,dB). Joint training is best among the tested
configurations. Decoder adaptation is therefore largely sufficient here, which does not show
it is necessary; a decoder-frozen counterpart was not trained. \textbf{Encoder geometry has no normalization-independent answer.} The
customary ratio $\lVert r\rVert^2/\lVert f(\bar{x})\rVert^2$ is translation-sensitive. The coefficients of $r=\bar{z}-f(\bar{x})$ sum to zero, so under
the relabelling of \secref{sec:prop} the numerator is unchanged while a
large enough $c$ inflates the denominator and drives the ratio toward zero.
\tabref{tab:latnorm} re-measures the three contrasts under four
normalizations on the held-out corpus. The unnormalized residual and the
ratio to the endpoint distance favour the mixing models throughout; the
ratio to each encoder's distance from its own $f(0)$ reverses at
$7.66\times$ and is indistinguishable from zero at $15.3\times$. All three
are translation-invariant. Recording the numerator and both denominators
separately is informative: with $D$ the latent element count, write
$N=\lVert r\rVert^2/D$, $S=\lVert f(x_1){-}f(x_2)\rVert^2/D$ and $O=\lVert
f(\bar{x}){-}f(0)\rVert^2/D$, each a squared quantity averaged over units.
Against its control, each mixing model has mean $N$ at $0.64$--$0.67$, mean
$S$ within $4\%$ at $7.66\times$ and $1.37$ at $15.3\times$, and mean $O$ at
$0.53$--$0.61$, so the residual falls by about a third while the mixture latents sit some
$22$--$27\%$ closer to $f(0)$ in RMS distance. These are means
of the parts, not of the ratio, so they do not by themselves determine the
mean ratio; the reversal of $N/O$ at $7.66\times$ is consistent with the
stronger reduction in its denominator. Both normalized ratios, and the customary one, are additionally invariant to
rescaling $f\!\to\!sf$ with the decoder adjusted, since numerator and
denominator scale alike; only the unnormalized residual is not. Scale
invariance therefore cannot decide between the two denominators. We therefore make no claim about
improved encoder geometry. \begin{table}[t] \centering\small

column is a treatment against its \emph{own no-mixing control} at the same
rate, so the $\Ldec{+}$disc column compares that recipe with no mixing, not

\subsection{Decoder consistency can misrepresent mixing quality}\label{sec:consistency}
Adding an adversarial term to the mixed path does \emph{not} improve
ground-truth equivariance ($10.2\!\to\!9.9$\,dB, MixRate $0.97\!\to\!1.05$),
yet it raises the decode-vs-decode SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$ from $12.1$ to
$16.0$\,dB. The held-out corpus confirms the direction and sharpens it: the
adversarial term \emph{costs} $0.49$\,dB $[-0.51,-0.48]$ of ground-truth
equivariance while adding $+4.06$\,dB of decode-consistency. The same divergence shows in the waveform: the adversarial term yields a
slightly \emph{smaller} latent residual ($0.0045$ against $0.0047$ per
element) yet a larger interpolation penalty ($0.52$ against $0.07$\,dB). The
measurements therefore rank the same checkpoints differently and cannot
serve as interchangeable evidence of mixing capability. We do not identify the
mechanism; one candidate is that both decodes are driven onto a common
realistic manifold. Ground-truth SI-SDR for the mixed-input reconstruction route is reported in
\secref{sec:mixing} for the held-out corpus, and MixRate scores that route
against $\bar{x}$ under the mixed-path loss; whether the discriminator acts
alone is untested.

\subsection{Subtraction depends strongly on the latent origin}\label{sec:origin}
We remove a stem by latent subtraction and measure SI-SDR against the true
residual on 49 of the 50 MUSDB18 test tracks (one is excluded because its
stems sum to the mixture at only $18.5$\,dB). This is an \emph{oracle} setting: the removed stem is encoded, so it tests
arithmetic, not blind separation. By \secref{sec:prop} we also report
$g(f(x_\mathrm{mix}){-}f(x_\mathrm{stem}){+}f(0))$.

\textbf{Raw subtraction favours the mixing loss; recentering substantially
reduces the difference.} Raw, $\Ldec$ raises subtraction quality at
$7.66\times$ from $+3.4$ to $+5.8$\,dB, and the matched $15.3\times$ pair
from $+1.2$ to $+5.2$. Adding $f(0)$ gains $+2.0$ to $+6.0$\,dB for every
evaluated configuration, leaving each within about $0.5$\,dB of its own
reconstruction reference in aggregate; per track the tail is wider. The
between-model difference then falls to $-0.03$\,dB (95\% track-cluster CI
$[-0.15,+0.10]$) and $+0.01$ $([-0.19,+0.21])$.

Because these checkpoints were selected on this same split
(\secref{sec:setup}), we repeat the intervention on MoisesDB, 240 recordings
absent from training and from selection, under a protocol registered before
the audio was downloaded. Raw subtraction again favours the mixing models by
$+2.3$ to $+4.5$\,dB and recentering removes $87$--$95$\% of that, shrinking
the gap on $229$--$236$ of the 240 recordings; on the rest it overshoots and
reverses the sign. The residue is resolvable there ($+0.29$, $+0.12$ and
$+0.42$\,dB, intervals excluding zero), so a small training-associated
advantage survives correction. Units are scored where the removed stem is active, at least $-50$\,dBFS and
within $30$\,dB of the mixture energy ($24{,}648$ of $28{,}800$ retained;
vocals retain the least, $70.4\%$). The rule excludes silent \emph{targets} and does
not make the SI-SDR reference nonsilent, that reference being the remaining
mixture. The protocol was committed before the audio was downloaded and is
released as \texttt{HOLDOUT\_PROTOCOL.md} with the checkpoint hashes the
evaluation verifies at run time.

\textbf{Baselines without latent arithmetic.} The unchanged mixture scores
$+17.1$\,dB against the residual, above every model's reconstruction
reference, since the removed stem is a small share of the energy: the metric
certifies arithmetic relative to that reference, not removal. Recentered
subtraction beats decode-then-subtract by $+0.7$ to $+0.9$\,dB; raw
subtraction loses to it.

\subsection{Scope}\label{sec:scope}
\tabref{tab:crossarch} extends the comparison. \textbf{Compression:} trained
from scratch at $15.3\times$ against a matched control, the combined
$\Ldec{+}$disc recipe improves SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ from $6.8$
to $8.6$\,dB and MixRate from $1.19$ to $1.05$ at equal reconstruction, while
FAD rises from $0.052$ to $0.061$; whether that is perceptible is untested.
That pair differs by the whole recipe and, unlike the fine-tunes, does not
share initial weights, so it is an observation about the combined recipe in
those two runs rather than an isolated effect of $\Ldec$. It appears on all three test domains at both rates ($+1.2$ to $+3.2$\,dB). \textbf{Level:} the decoded mix is $1.6$\,dB too quiet without the loss and
$0.9$ with it, against a reconstruction level error of $0.8$\,dB.
\textbf{Architecture:} we ask whether the benefit extends to another
autoencoder family, applying the same mixed-latent supervision to
Music2Latent \cite{pasini2024music2latent} under its native consistency
loss. Mixing SI-SDR improves against a
continued-training control ($-9.5\!\to\!-7.8$\,dB), which itself moves
equivariance by $<0.2$\,dB, while absolute waveform performance remains poor.
The systems differ in too many respects for this to isolate a cause, and we
claim nothing about consistency autoencoders in general.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Explicit mixing supervision improves ground-truth waveform mixing in the
evaluated GAN autoencoders, with little change in reconstruction SI-SDR, and
the improvement holds on a held-out corpus that took no part in training or
in checkpoint selection. Decoder consistency and stem subtraction, however,
report different properties of the same checkpoints: an adversarial term raises decode-consistency while lowering ground-truth
mixing, and most of the raw subtraction advantage disappears when each
checkpoint is recentered at its silence latent. The three measurements are
therefore not interchangeable evidence of mixing capability.
\textbf{Limitations.} Comparisons use single training runs: fine-tunes share
a starting checkpoint, the from-scratch pair only a configuration, as
training sets no explicit seed. Intervals cover recording-level evaluation
variation conditional on these checkpoints, not variation across training
runs. Selection used the test split; audio is mono and untested by
listeners.
