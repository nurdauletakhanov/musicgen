\title{What Makes Audio Latents Mixing-Equivariant?\\A Controlled Study of Explicit Supervision}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Nurdaulet Akhanov}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence\\
Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates\\
nurdaulet.akhanov@mbzuai.ac.ae}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Mixing is linear on waveforms, so a latent space in which interpolating two
codes and decoding reproduces the waveform mix is an \emph{equivariant}
representation. Recent work induces this by decoding averaged latents
against the true mix. We ask which part of that supervision is responsible
and whether the certifying metrics measure it at all. Decode-mixing supervision improves mixing equivariance with little change in
reconstruction SI-SDR; the combined recipe also improves mixing at twice the
compression. An adversarial term on the mixed path adds no
ground-truth gain while inflating a decode-consistency metric. Latent stem
subtraction, in contrast, is governed largely by the latent origin: adding
the encoded silence $f(0)$ reduces the loss's apparent subtraction advantage
by $87$--$95$\% on a pre-registered $240$-track held-out corpus. Comparisons
are single-run and matched at one initialization.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
representation learning, equivariant representations, audio autoencoders,
latent arithmetic, controlled ablation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Audio mixing is a linear operation:
$\mathrm{mix}(x_1,x_2,\alpha)=\alpha x_1+(1{-}\alpha)x_2$.
For an autoencoder with encoder $f$ and decoder $g$, we call the latent space
\emph{mixing-equivariant} when
\begin{equation}
  g\!\left(\alpha f(x_1)+(1{-}\alpha)f(x_2)\right)\approx \alpha x_1+(1{-}\alpha)x_2 .
  \label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv} constrains combinations whose coefficients sum to one, so
latent \emph{subtraction} $g(f(x_\mathrm{mix}){-}f(x_\mathrm{stem}))$, with
coefficients $(1,-1)$, is not implied by it: an offset $f(x)=x+b$, $g(z)=z-b$
satisfies Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv} exactly yet subtracts to $x_\mathrm{res}-b$. For
an affine $f(x)=Ax+b$, adding the encoded silence restores the sum to one and
$f(x_\mathrm{mix}){-}f(x_\mathrm{stem}){+}f(0)=f(x_\mathrm{res})$, so the decode
is the model's own reconstruction of the residual, whatever the decoder;
matching the residual waveform additionally requires exact reconstruction. How
far real encoders depart from affine is empirical
(\secref{sec:downstream}). Where it holds,
latent arithmetic becomes audio editing without a dedicated separation or
synthesis model, and it is a prerequisite for latent-diffusion systems
\cite{liu2023audioldm,evans2024stable} that compose independently modeled
sources. Yet standard autoencoders do not satisfy
Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv}: their objectives reward reconstruction fidelity, not
algebraic structure, and in quantized codecs (DAC \cite{kumar2024dac}, EnCodec
\cite{defossez2023encodec}) an interpolated point is not a codebook entry,
so code-domain mixing is undefined without leaving the quantizer.

Torres et al.~\cite{torres2026linearity} induce linearity in a consistency
autoencoder by decoding the \emph{average} of separately encoded latents and
training it to reconstruct the true mix, a supervision principle related to
mixup \cite{zhang2018mixup}. This leaves open which part of that supervision
is responsible for the property, whether the encoder or the decoder must carry
it, whether a consistency decoder can turn latent linearity into usable
waveform arithmetic, and whether the metrics used to certify it measure it at
all. We answer these questions with a controlled study: the same principle
written as an explicit loss (\secref{sec:method}) on a waveform GAN
autoencoder, whose effect we then isolate by intervening on one factor at a
time. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A controlled study of mixed-latent supervision on a waveform GAN
  autoencoder: the decode-mixing loss alone is sufficient, an adversarial term
  on the mixed path adds no ground-truth gain on the tested contrast, and
decoder-only adaptation retains most of the improvement while joint
adaptation is best among the tested configurations (\secref{sec:ablation}).
  \item Generalization against matched controls: the combined
  $\Ldec{+}$disc recipe trained from scratch at $2\times$ tighter compression,
  and a re-run on a consistency-model
  autoencoder where latent geometry improves but waveform arithmetic does not
  (\secref{sec:general}).
  \item A downstream analysis showing latent stem removal is limited by the
  latent origin rather than the loss, for every evaluated GAN configuration
  (\secref{sec:downstream}).
  \item A methodological correction: the widely used decode-vs-decode
  SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$ is sensitive to the decode-noise protocol and to an
adversarial term; we report a ground-truth-referenced variant.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Neural audio codecs.} DAC \cite{kumar2024dac},
EnCodec \cite{defossez2023encodec}, and SoundStream \cite{zeghidour2022soundstream}
pair convolutional encoders with HiFi-GAN decoders \cite{kong2020hifigan} and
multi-scale discriminators, optimizing reconstruction without latent structure;
their residual vector quantization also precludes latent interpolation.
RAVE \cite{caillon2021rave} learns a variational latent space but does not
target mixing equivariance, and disentanglement regularizers
\cite{higgins2017betavae} encourage generic smoothness rather than a specific
algebraic identity.
\textbf{Equivariant representations.} Mixing-equivariance follows the
symmetry-based view of representation learning \cite{higgins2018towards},
though convex mixing is an affine combination, not a group action, so we
borrow the framing and not the formalism. We treat equivariance as a property to be
\emph{induced} by supervision and isolate which supervision induces it.
\textbf{Linear audio representations.} Music2Latent \cite{pasini2024music2latent}
is a consistency autoencoder with partial linearity as a byproduct.
Torres et al.~\cite{torres2026linearity} make it approximately linear by
conditioning the decoder on the average of separately encoded latents while
denoising the true mix under the unchanged consistency loss (plus a random
gain for homogeneity, which we do not study). Our $\Ldec$ is the explicit-loss
form of that additivity supervision: the same input (averaged latents) and
target (true mix), differing in loss family (multi-resolution STFT and mel
versus consistency), mixing coefficient ($\alpha\sim U[0,1]$ versus $0.5$),
and decoder class (deterministic GAN versus consistency model). We add the
analysis: which ingredient carries the effect, a matched re-run on their
architecture with a continued-training control (\secref{sec:general}), and
ground-truth-referenced metrics. Their additivity (SNR, spectral distance) and
separation scores compare two decodes, the protocol \secref{sec:metrics} shows
to be confounded, and their separation is negative on every stem ($-0.3$ to
$-1.8$\,dB), consistent with this sampling sensitivity.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Decode-mixing loss}\label{sec:loss}
Given a batch $\{x_i\}$ we form pairs $(x_i,x_j)$ with a fixed-point-free
permutation and draw $\alpha\sim U[0,1]$. Let $\bar{x}=\alpha x_i+(1{-}\alpha)x_j$
be the waveform mix and $\bar{z}=\alpha f(x_i)+(1{-}\alpha)f(x_j)$ the latent
interpolation. The decode-mixing loss requires the decoded interpolation to
match the true mix:
\begin{equation}
  \Ldec=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}\!\left(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x}\right)
+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{wave}\text{-}\ell_1}\!\left(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x}\right),
\label{eq:ldec} \end{equation} where $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}$ is the weighted multi-resolution STFT and
mel loss also used for reconstruction and
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{wave}\text{-}\ell_1}$ is the unweighted mean absolute
error over all samples and batch elements. The reconstruction path carries
no waveform term, so the two paths differ in objective. Gradients flow through $g$ and back into $f$ via
$\bar{z}$, so a single term constrains both networks; it costs one extra
decoder pass per step. We also study an \emph{encoder-only} variant that
penalizes encoder nonlinearity directly,
$\Lenc=\lVert f(\bar{x})-\bar{z}\rVert^2$ (one extra encoder pass, no decoder
pass). The total generator objective is
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{recon}+\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{GAN}+\lambda\,\Ldec
+\lambda_{\ell_2}\lVert z\rVert^2$.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
We report reconstruction SI-SDR$_\mathrm{rec}=\mathrm{SISDR}(g(f(x)),x)$ and
two equivariance views at $\alpha{=}0.5$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$} (decode-vs-decode):
  $\mathrm{SISDR}(g(\bar{z}),g(f(\bar{x})))$, equivariance relative to the
  model's \emph{own} reconstruction of the mix.
  \item \textbf{SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$} (vs.\ ground truth):
  $\mathrm{SISDR}(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x})$, the quantity in
  Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv} up to scale, comparable across models. SI-SDR fits a gain before scoring, so it does not assess level preservation;
we report the fitted gain separately (\secref{sec:general}). FAD uses music-finetuned LAION-CLAP embeddings of non-overlapping 10\,s windows at 48\,kHz,
not VGGish, so the values are not comparable with VGGish-based FAD; the
fitted gain is measured on 3{,}000 samples.
\end{itemize}
We also report $\ell_\mathrm{lat}=\lVert\bar{z}-f(\bar{x})\rVert^2/\lVert f(\bar{x})\rVert^2$
(encoder linearity) and MixRate
$=\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{mix}(g(\bar{z}),\bar{x})/\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{mix}(g(f(\bar{x})),\bar{x})$,
with $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{mix}$ the three-term mixed-path loss above in both
numerator and denominator,
(decoder equivariance; $<1$ is better than the encode-then-decode path).
SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$ shares the decode-vs-decode structure of prior work's
decoder-additivity metrics (SNR and spectral distance between two decodes
\cite{torres2026linearity}), and the two views can be moved apart: an adversarial term on the mixed path
raises the mutual SI-SDR of the two decode paths without bringing either
closer to $\bar{x}$ (\secref{sec:ablation}). We do not identify the
mechanism; one candidate is that both decodes are driven onto a common
realistic manifold. It is also confounded for stochastic
decoders: a consistency-model decoder \cite{pasini2024music2latent} draws fresh
noise per call. Decoding the \emph{same} latent twice is deterministic under
shared noise but scores $-1.8$\,dB under independent noise: decoder sampling
substantially affects decode-vs-decode scores even when no latent arithmetic is
performed. On identical pairs the same Music2Latent checkpoints score $-9.1$ and
$-7.3$\,dB when the two decodes draw
independent noise and $+3.6$ and $+5.9$\,dB when they share it, a $13$\,dB
swing driven by the evaluation protocol rather than the model. Which
protocol prior work used is not stated in enough detail for us to determine. This shows protocol sensitivity; decoding
the same latent twice with independent noise isolates how much of the
disagreement arises without any latent arithmetic (\secref{sec:general}). We therefore treat
SI-SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ and MixRate as the equivariance metrics of
record, and use shared decode noise everywhere.


\subsection{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}
The primary model is a waveform autoencoder (DAC-style 1-D convolutional
encoder, HiFi-GAN decoder \cite{kong2020hifigan}, combined multi-scale-STFT and
multi-period discriminator) operating at 44.1\,kHz on 1-second mono chunks,
trained on a ${\sim}$950-hour union of FMA-large, MAESTRO, and MUSDB18-HQ
\cite{rafii2017musdb18}.\footnote{Code, training configurations, pretrained
weights, every metric reported here, and audio examples:
\url{https://github.com/nurdauletakhanov/musicgen}} Two compression regimes are used: $7.66\times$
($d{=}128$) and $15.3\times$ ($d{=}64$). Mixing losses are added either as a
25k-step fine-tune over a converged baseline (v2.x) or from scratch over 250k
steps (v3.x). \textbf{Checkpoint selection.} The released weights are the
checkpoint with the lowest validation training loss, and the validation loader
read the test split; for every v2.x run this coincides with the final step, so
selection did not alter those weights, while both v3.x runs were selected at
step 240k of 250k. Contrasts are therefore between matched configurations
under identical selection, but absolute generalization claims should be read
with this in mind; the code now supports a held-out validation split. All runs use a single NVIDIA RTX~5090. Unless noted, metrics are
computed over the full multi-source test set; the $\alpha$-sweep
(\secref{sec:alpha}) uses a source-balanced subsample.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Which signal causes equivariance? An interventional ablation}\label{sec:ablation}
\tabref{tab:ablation} ablates the loss on the $7.66\times$ GAN autoencoder
(25k-step fine-tunes from a shared converged baseline). Each row differs from
its comparator in exactly one factor: $\Ldec$ and $\Lenc$ against the
baseline (the loss), $\Ldec{+}$disc against $\Ldec$ (the adversarial term),
and frozen-encoder $\Ldec$ against $\Ldec$ (the encoder's freedom to adapt);
the design is not a full factorial. Three observations drive the paper.

\textbf{(1) Reconstruction is similar to the matched control at this rate.}
SDR$_\mathrm{rec}$ spans $11.2$--$11.4$\,dB and FAD $0.044$--$0.046$, so we
observe no reconstruction cost at $7.66\times$. These are single runs, so an
absence this small is not established beyond seed variance.

\textbf{(2) The decode-mixing loss is the active ingredient.} On the ground-truth metric, $\Ldec$ alone improves
SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ from $8.0$ to $10.2$\,dB and is the only run with
MixRate $<1$ ($0.97$). Adding an adversarial term to the mixed path
(``+disc-on-mix'') does \emph{not} improve ground-truth equivariance
($9.9$\,dB, MixRate $1.05$), yet it dramatically inflates the decode-vs-decode
SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$ ($12.1\!\to\!16.0$\,dB). The two decode paths move toward each other without either moving toward
$\bar{x}$; one candidate mechanism is a common realistic manifold. This is direct evidence that
SDR$_\mathrm{lin}$ can rise without ground-truth equivariance rising, and
that given $\Ldec$ no adversarial term improved the ground-truth metric
here; whether the discriminator has an effect on its own, without $\Ldec$,
is not tested here.

\textbf{(3) Decoder-only adaptation retains most of the improvement.} The
encoder-only variant $\Lenc$ drives $\ell_\mathrm{lat}$ lowest but leaves
MixRate high ($1.15$--$1.18$): it linearizes the encoder without making the
decoder equivariant. Training only the decoder under $\Ldec$ retains most of
the gain ($9.5$\,dB versus $10.2$ joint and $8.0$ baseline) while leaving
encoder linearity unimproved. Joint training is best among the tested
configurations. That decoder adaptation is largely sufficient here does not
show it is necessary, which would need a decoder-frozen counterpart we did
not train.

\subsection{Intervening on compression and architecture}\label{sec:general}
\tabref{tab:crossarch} extends the recipe along two axes. \textbf{Compression:}
trained \emph{from scratch} at $15.3\times$ (v3.1, which uses the combined
$\Ldec{+}$disc recipe, so it tests that combination rather than $\Ldec$ alone),
the mixing model matches its
no-mixing control (v3.0) on reconstruction ($9.9$ vs.\ $10.0$\,dB) while
improving ground-truth equivariance by $+1.7$\,dB
(SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ $6.8\!\to\!8.6$), MixRate ($1.19\!\to\!1.05$),
and encoder linearity ($\ell_\mathrm{lat}$ $0.078\!\to\!0.057$). FAD, however, rises from $0.052$ to $0.061$ (\tabref{tab:crossarch});
whether that is perceptible is untested. At $7.66\times$ the reported
reconstruction metrics are similar in these runs (\tabref{tab:ablation}); at
$15.3\times$ that similarity holds for SDR$_\mathrm{rec}$ only. Because v3.0 is a matched control, the effect is not an artifact of the
looser rate or of warm-starting.
\textbf{Level preservation.} The gain SI-SDR discards: the decoded mix is
$1.6$\,dB too quiet without the loss and $0.9$\,dB with it, against a
reconstruction level error of $0.8$\,dB. The loss reduces level error as
well as spectral error. \textbf{Architecture:} we fine-tune the published Music2Latent
\cite{pasini2024music2latent} consistency autoencoder with the same mixed-latent supervision under its native consistency loss. A continued-training
control moves equivariance by $<0.2$\,dB and \emph{worsens} FAD, so the gain
is the loss, not domain adaptation. In our Music2Latent fine-tuning experiment, mixing supervision reduces
latent mixing error and improves ground-truth mixing SI-SDR
($-9.5\!\to\!-7.8$\,dB), while waveform agreement remains poor. The systems
differ in too many respects for this to isolate a cause, and we make no
claim about consistency autoencoders in general.

\subsection{Downstream: latent-arithmetic stem removal}\label{sec:downstream}
We remove a stem by latent subtraction and measure SI-SDR against the true
residual on 49 of the 50 MUSDB18 test tracks (one is excluded because its
stems sum to the mixture at only $18.5$\,dB). This is an \emph{oracle}
setting: the removed stem is encoded, so it tests arithmetic, not blind
separation. Subtraction uses coefficients $(1,-1)$, which Eq.~\ref{eq:equiv} does not
cover, so we also report the origin-corrected form
$g(f(x_\mathrm{mix})-f(x_\mathrm{stem})+f(0))$. For an affine encoder this
recovers the residual's latent exactly and so decodes
$g(f(x_\mathrm{res}))$, the model's own reconstruction of the residual; the
shortfall against that reference is the \emph{linearity tax}.

\textbf{Raw subtraction favours the mixing loss; origin correction
substantially reduces the difference.} Raw, $\Ldec$ raises subtraction quality at $7.66\times$
($+3.4\!\to\!+5.8$\,dB) and the matched $15.3\times$ pair goes from $+1.2$ to
$+5.2$\,dB. Adding $f(0)$ gains $+2.0$ to $+6.0$\,dB for every evaluated GAN
configuration, leaving each within about $0.5$\,dB of that reference in
aggregate (\tabref{tab:subtraction}); per track the tail is wider. For these autoencoders, origin correction removes most of the mean
subtraction advantage of mixing supervision: $\Ldec$ versus no mixing is
$-0.03$\,dB (95\% track-cluster CI $[-0.15,+0.10]$), the $15.3\times$ pair
$+0.01$ ($[-0.19,+0.21]$). Because these checkpoints were selected on this
same split (\secref{sec:setup}), we repeat the intervention on MoisesDB
\cite{pereira2023moisesdb}, 240 recordings absent from training and from
selection, under a protocol registered before the audio was downloaded. Raw subtraction again favours the mixing models by $+2.3$ to $+4.5$\,dB and
recentering removes $87$--$95$\% of that, shrinking the gap on $229$--$236$
of the $240$ recordings, but the residue is resolvable there ($+0.29$,
$+0.12$, $+0.42$\,dB, intervals excluding zero). Convex mixing replicates on
the same corpus: $\Ldec$ gains $+1.73$\,dB $[+1.69,+1.78]$ at $7.66\times$
and the combined recipe $+1.33$ $[+1.27,+1.39]$ at $15.3\times$ for a
reconstruction change of $+0.04$ and $-0.07$\,dB, while the adversarial term
\emph{costs} $0.49$\,dB $[-0.51,-0.48]$ of ground-truth equivariance and
adds $+4.06$\,dB of decode-vs-decode. For the held-out subtraction only, units are scored where the removed stem
is active ($24{,}648$ of $28{,}800$); the rule excludes silent
\emph{targets} and does not make the SI-SDR reference nonsilent, that
reference being the remaining mixture. The three residual contrasts are
$\Ldec$ and $\Ldec{+}$disc against no mixing at $7.66\times$, and the
combined recipe against no mixing at $15.3\times$. The comparator figures
below return to the MUSDB18 test set. The no-mixing models also carry the larger offset ($\lVert f(0)\rVert$ $6.6$
versus $5.4$ at $7.66\times$), though offset norm alone does not determine
the decoded error. What
the loss buys is convex equivariance (\tabref{tab:ablation}), which the
correction leaves intact. The residual $0.4$--$0.5$\,dB is the arithmetic gap left after decoding, not
a direct measure of non-affinity.

\textbf{Baselines without latent arithmetic.} The unchanged mixture scores
$+17.1$\,dB against the residual, above every model's reconstruction
reference, since the removed stem is a small share of the energy: the metric
certifies arithmetic only relative to the model's own reconstruction.
Origin-corrected latent subtraction beats decode-then-subtract by $+0.7$ to
$+0.9$\,dB (CIs exclude zero); raw subtraction loses to it everywhere.

Intervals come from a paired cluster bootstrap over recordings ($10^4$
resamples), 49 tracks on the test set and 240 on the held-out corpus; mixing pairs join two chunks, about $80\%$ of them from the same recording,
and are weighted by the multiplicity of each member. They cover evaluation variance at the
tested initialization only.

\subsection{Equivariance across mixing ratios}\label{sec:alpha}
$\Ldec$ and its matched control are the pair swept under the same protocol
(source-balanced, 1{,}200 samples per coefficient). The
SDR$_\mathrm{lin}^\mathrm{gt}$ swing across $\alpha$ is $1.9$\,dB with
$\Ldec$ against $3.2$\,dB without, and $\Ldec$ leads at all five
coefficients (by $0.6$ to $1.9$\,dB), so the property is not specific to
$\alpha{=}0.5$. The $\Ldec{+}$disc sweep used the full test set, a different
population, so we do not compare it here.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A controlled study identifies the decode-mixing loss, not an adversarial
term on the mixed path, as the signal behind convex mixing-equivariance,
with reconstruction SI-SDR similar to the matched control at each rate.
Raw stem subtraction is instead governed largely by the latent offset:
adding encoded silence brings subtraction in aggregate within about $1$\,dB
of each model's reconstruction reference and reduces the loss's apparent
advantage by $87$--$95$\% on a held-out corpus. That percentage is
empirical; what the affine assumption guarantees is recovery of the
residual's latent, not the size of the reduction. In our Music2Latent fine-tuning experiment, mixing supervision reduces latent mixing error and
improves ground-truth mixing SI-SDR ($-9.5\!\to\!-7.8$\,dB), while waveform
agreement remains poor. \textbf{Limitations.} Comparisons are matched at one initialization, not
across runs; checkpoint selection used the test split (\secref{sec:setup});
audio is mono and untested by listeners.
